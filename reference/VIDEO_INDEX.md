# Palworld trailer — motion reference index

60 frames sampled every 2 s from the official Palworld 1.0 launch trailer, 1280 px wide,
in `reference/palworld_video/` (`tr_001.jpg` .. `tr_060.jpg`). All 60 were viewed directly.

This file is the **motion** half of the blind A/B gauntlet. `INDEX.md` covers still-frame
art direction; this one covers behaviour over time, because our motion instrument compares
6-frame strips, not single frames.

Our vertical slice is the **first five minutes**: grassy starting biome, wandering creatures,
no guns, no bases, no bosses. A trailer is the worst possible place to find that — it is
selling the endgame. The mismatch is stated plainly below rather than papered over.

## 2. Runs

The trailer cuts roughly every 2–4 s, which is the same as the sample rate. Consequence:
**almost no two adjacent frames are literally the same camera take.** What the set does give
is contiguous *blocks* — consecutive frames belonging to one edit sequence, one subject or one
location. Those are what is usable, and they are labelled as blocks, not as shots.

| Run | Frames | Length | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — opening flight** | `tr_002`–`tr_006` | ~10 s | One rider on one flying mount, follow-cam, cut through four biomes (grass ruins → snow → red autumn → coast) into the title. Same subject and same camera relationship throughout; the biome changes on the cuts. |
| **B — combat sampler** | `tr_009`–`tr_010`, `tr_014`–`tr_015` | ~4 s each pair | Four unrelated HUD firefights. Not a run in any useful sense — no continuity between them. |

## 3. Chosen motion comps

### `behaviour_idle` — creatures left alone for 12 s, no player interaction

**Primary comp: Run D, tightened to `tr_036`, `tr_037`, `tr_041`, `tr_042`.**

These are the only frames in the set where a *population* of creatures is visible with the
camera not pointed at any one of them. In `tr_042`, roughly eight Pals share the hillside and
no two are in the same pose or the same phase: two are lying flat, one hovers, one is
mid-stride uphill, one is slumped over a workbench, one is carrying something. `tr_036` does
the same with ten. That "zero pose collisions in a crowd" property is exactly what a 6-frame
idle strip either has or does not have, and it is the fastest thing for a critic to check.

**Secondary comp (single-creature body language): `tr_012` + `tr_028` + `tr_045` + `tr_007`.**
Four separate 2 s beauty shots of one unengaged creature, treated as a synthetic set. Every
one is caught in an asymmetric, non-neutral, clearly-mid-something pose — never a symmetric
breathing idle. Use these for what a single resting silhouette should look like at any
given instant of the strip.

**Honest caveat.** Base Pals have literal assigned jobs, so "own agenda" is being handed to
them by the game's systems rather than emerging from wild behaviour. And no shot in the
trailer holds on unengaged wild creatures for 12 s — the closest is `tr_027`, a 2 s wide of
five wild Pals flying and perched at different depths. So Run D is a fair comp for
**distribution, variance and simultaneity**, and is *not* a fair comp for what a wild
creature does when nothing needs doing. Judge that half on our own rubric.

### `behaviour_approach` — player walks toward a creature; notice → assess → react

**No fair comp exists in these 60 frames. Do not force one.**

Every wild-creature encounter in this trailer is either already a firefight (`tr_009`,
`tr_010`, `tr_014`, `tr_015`, `tr_031`, `tr_032`) or a pre-posed beauty shot with the
creature already aware of and playing to the camera (`tr_012`, `tr_019`, `tr_028`, `tr_045`).
The arc we are testing — a creature that does not know you are there, notices, assesses, then
decides — is never shown, because a trailer has no reason to spend six seconds on it.

**Nearest partials, useful only for geometry and endpoint states, not for the arc:**

- `tr_016` — player crossing open sand toward a Pal that is *already leaving* on its own
  errand. This is the single most useful frame for the approach setup: the creature has
  somewhere to be, and the player is the one closing distance.
- `tr_025`, `tr_043` — the endpoint. Player at contact distance; the creature is relaxed,
  not squared up, not fleeing, not aggro'd. This is what "reacted and settled" should
  look like, and it is notably *undramatic*.
- `tr_018` — a small Pal trotting at an NPC's heel, matching his pace. The only frame showing
  a creature actively tracking a human's movement over time.
- `tr_056` — a wild Pal walking down a path toward the camera at dusk. Closing distance, but
  the creature is the one approaching, not the player.

Recommendation: grade `behaviour_approach` against the acceptance criteria in section 4,
items 5, 8 and 11, rather than against any image in this set.

## 4. WHAT ALIVENESS LOOKS LIKE IN MOTION

Acceptance criteria for the behaviour system. Each item is written to be implementable and is
tied to the frames it was read off. Where a claim is inferred across cuts rather than seen
within one take, it says so.

**1. Zero pose collisions in a crowd.** `tr_042` has ~8 Pals and `tr_036` has ~10; in both,
no two creatures share a pose, a phase, or a facing. Spec: per instance, randomise animation
phase offset across the full 0–100 % of the cycle, *and* draw from at least four genuinely
distinct idle states. Two instances of the same species must be visibly out of sync within
3 s of spawning. Synchronised idle across a group is the single loudest tell of a fake world
and will be the first thing the critic sees in a 6-frame strip.

**2. Idle means "occupying a point of interest", not "standing and breathing".** Every idle
creature in this set is engaged with a *thing*: `tr_035` a fox Pal lying prone aiming a flame
jet at one specific campfire; `tr_044` four Pals converging on one fruit basket at four
different angles and reaches; `tr_042` one Pal slumped over a workbench, another lying in
grass. Spec: an idle creature must own a target — a grass tuft, a rock, a puddle, another
creature — and its animation must be *about* that target. No creature in a strip should be
standing in open ground doing nothing to anything.

**3. Settle into a new idle pose every 2–4 s; never loop one cycle.** Read across `tr_012`,
`tr_028`, `tr_045` and `tr_007`: every resting creature is caught in an asymmetric,
non-neutral pose — head cocked 20–30° off body axis, one ear forward and one back, weight on
three of four limbs, eyes half-lidded rather than open. Spec: 0.3–0.5 s eased transition into
a new hold, then hold 2–4 s, and **do not pass through a neutral pose between holds**. Over a
12 s strip that is 3–5 distinct silhouettes for a single creature. A strip where frames 1 and
6 are the same silhouette has failed.

**4. Full body weight sits on the ground.** `tr_012`: the round Pal's mass is settled *into*
the grass and blades cross its silhouette. `tr_043`: the alpaca's wool deforms under the
player's hand. `tr_044`: the Pals flatten where they contact the picnic blanket. `tr_036`:
every Pal has a tight, near-black contact shadow directly under it. Spec: idles carry a
2–4 cm vertical compression on the support limbs, contact AO is tight and dark, and grass,
cloth and water deform at the contact point. A creature hovering 2 cm above its shadow is
dead on arrival.

**5. Head and eyes lead the body, then hold.** `tr_012` head turned away from camera to
screen-right with the leaf-ears trailing the turn; `tr_045` head levelled straight down the
lens with ears vertical; `tr_007` head thrown fully back mid-call. Spec: eyes lead the head by
~0.1 s, head leads the body by 0.15–0.3 s, and once the head reaches its target it **holds
0.5–1.5 s** before the body commits or the head returns. Attention priority: moving player >
nearest active creature > moving prop (water, fire, falling leaf) > random ambient point every
3–6 s. A head that tracks continuously without holds reads as a turret, not an animal.

**6. Ears, tails, wool and crests lag and overshoot.** `tr_045` holds two long ears vertical
but with a soft S-curve in them; `tr_012`'s leaf-ears and tail are visibly out of phase with
the body; `tr_043`'s alpaca wool moves as one soft mass rather than a rigid shell; `tr_019`
shows the running bird Pal's crest swept back behind the head. Spec: 2–4 bone chains per
appendage, 0.1–0.2 s lag behind the parent, 1–2 overshoot oscillations damping over ~0.6 s.
**Every head turn must produce visible ear travel.** If the ears rotate rigidly with the
skull, the turn reads as a mesh rotation and the whole creature dies with it.

**7. Locomotion bounces, pitches and squashes.** `tr_019` (bird Pal running at camera, body
pitched forward into the run), `tr_029` (monkey Pal mid-stride with arms counter-swinging and
the torso rolled toward the planted foot), `tr_039` (umbrella Pal leaning into a turn past
camera). Spec: one vertical bob per stride at 3–6 cm, 3–8 % squash on foot plant and stretch
at apex, 10–20° forward body pitch while moving, limbs counter-rotating with the hips.
Footfalls must land at the bottom of the bob and kick up dust or bend grass — `tr_016` shows
the drag trail the departing Pal leaves in the sand.

**8. Starts and stops are events, not switches.** `tr_016` catches the Pal mid-departure with
a trail already behind it while the player is still accelerating. Spec: 0.2–0.4 s acceleration
ramp in which the **lean precedes the translation**, and a 0.3–0.5 s deceleration with a small
overshoot and settle at the end. A creature that is at full speed on frame 1 of the strip and
at zero on frame 2 is a prop being teleported. This is the item most likely to break
`behaviour_approach`.

**9. Creatures relate to each other, not only to the player.** `tr_044`: four Pals share one
basket, one leaning in over another's shoulder. `tr_036`/`tr_037`: Pals queue single-file
along a path and cluster around the same tub. `tr_007`: a small Pal perched on the big one's
shoulder. `tr_027`: wild flyers spaced at clearly distinct depths rather than stacked. Spec:
at least one pairwise relationship per group — a follower holding 1.5–3 m offset, a "watcher"
that orients toward whichever neighbour is most active, and ~1 m personal-space repulsion.
Across a 6-frame strip the group must visibly **re-form**; a group in fixed formation across
all six frames has failed.

**10. Groups are staggered in scale, facing and rate.** `tr_042` and `tr_036` mix small,
medium and large Pals at different distances, facing four or five different directions.
`tr_018` puts a small Pal at an NPC's heel and two tall Pals standing off to one side. Spec:
never spawn a group facing the same way — randomise facing over ±180°, spacing over 1.5–4 m,
and per-instance animation rate by ±10 %. Identical species must drift out of sync inside 3 s.

**11. Approach reaction is graded by distance and takes seconds, not frames.** Inferred, since
the trailer never shows the full arc — but the endpoints are visible: `tr_016` a Pal that
simply keeps leaving on its own errand while a human closes; `tr_025` and `tr_043` a creature
at contact distance that is relaxed and *not* squared up to the player. Spec: beyond ~15 m,
no reaction at all. At 8–10 m, an ears-and-head-only orient lasting 0.5–1 s while the body
continues its previous activity. At ~5 m, the body commits to one of three legible outcomes —
turn and face, reposition 1–3 m away, or resume the previous activity with occasional
glance-backs. Only inside ~2 m does a contact-level reaction fire. The whole arc runs 3–6 s
and must never snap between states; each transition needs its own 0.3 s blend. Crucially, the
most common correct outcome is the *undramatic* one: the creature notices you and goes back to
what it was doing.

**12. The camera and the world move too.** Every creature shot here runs 2–4 s, sits at or
just below the creature's eye height on a longer lens with shallow depth (`tr_012`, `tr_028`,
`tr_045`), and drifts slowly laterally or pushes in rather than locking off. And there is
always secondary world motion at a second depth: grass blades crossing the silhouette in
`tr_012`, splash and surface ripple in `tr_028`, blowing sand in `tr_016`, waterfalls and
drifting particles at four depths in `tr_027`, falling blossom in `tr_045`. Spec for our
capture strips: camera at creature eye height, a slow constant drift so parallax is doing work
in every frame, the creature framed off-centre with somewhere to move to, and **at least two
non-creature things moving at different depths in every frame of the strip**.
