# Palworld reference set — classification and matched comps

17 official Palworld screenshots, 1920x1080, in `reference/palworld/`.
All 17 were viewed directly. This file is the reference half of the blind A/B gauntlet:
it says which official frame each of our six shots is fairly compared against, and what
an artist should actually change to close the gap.

Our vertical slice is **the first five minutes** of a Palworld-like: grassy starting biome,
wandering creatures, no guns, no bases, no bosses. Most of the official set is *not* that.
That mismatch is called out honestly below rather than papered over.

## 3. Gaps

Shot types we have **no fair reference for**. Grade these against our own intent, not against a forced comp.

1. **True golden hour.** Not one frame in the set is shot at low warm sun. Lighting here is
   overwhelmingly high-key midday (`pw_02`, `pw_08`, `pw_13`, `pw_15`) or night/blue-hour
   (`pw_09`, `pw_16`). `vista_golden` has no like-for-like comp for colour temperature.
2. **Peaceful player+creature interaction.** No feeding, petting, taming or offering frame exists.
   `interaction_feed` is unmatched.
3. **Wild creatures wandering an unbuilt meadow.** Every multi-creature frame has creatures either
   working (`pw_06`), fighting (`pw_00`, `pw_13`), or in a built settlement (`pw_07`, `pw_09`, `pw_16`).
   `creature_group` has a framing comp but no *situational* comp.
4. **The actual first five minutes.** No frame shows an unequipped player in the starting grassland.
   Every player-visible shot has late-game kit: powered armour, firearms, or a mount.
5. **Unbuilt landscape.** Every wide shot contains a man-made landmark (obelisk, chapel, base, village).
   If our vista is pure nature, that is a deliberate divergence, not a failure.

## 4. What makes these frames read as Palworld

Concrete, actionable, drawn from the frames above. This is the art-direction brief.

1. **Creature bodies are one dominant primitive, not an assembled skeleton.**
   The torso is a sphere or a rounded egg that accounts for **55–75% of total height**, and the head is
   *fused into* that mass rather than sitting on a neck (`pw_00` sheep, `pw_09` pair, `pw_15` fox).
   Limbs are stubby nubs at **15–20% of body height** with no visible elbow or knee, ending in mitten
   hands and toeless feet. If your creature has a discernible neck, waist, or limb joints, it has
   already stopped reading as a Pal.

2. **All silhouette complexity lives in appendages, and it overshoots the body.**
   The body outline is smooth and convex; the *only* high-frequency features are ears, horns, crests
   and tails, and they are enormous — the ears in `pw_09` are nearly as tall as the entire body, the
   fox ears in `pw_15` project a full head-width past the skull, the wool in `pw_00` reads as a ring of
   uniform puffball lobes. Budget your silhouette detail there and nowhere else. The whole shape must
   be identifiable as a black-on-white cutout at 100 px tall.

3. **Faces are flat vector graphics laid on a 3D form.**
   Eyes are huge, hard-edged, with pure-white sclera, a thick dark outline, a fully saturated iris
   (cyan `pw_15`, blue `pw_12`, yellow `pw_00`) and exactly one round specular dot. The mouth is one
   simple curve. Facial features sit on a **flat or slightly recessed front plane** of the head sphere
   and frequently on a distinct colour patch (the sheep's brown face inside white wool). No sculpted
   nose, no lip geometry, no eyelid modelling — this is drawn, not modelled.

4. **Fur is matte and micro-fuzzy; nothing is glossy.**
   The pink fox (`pw_15`) and the wool (`pw_00`) read as fine flocked noise with a **broad, very low
   intensity diffuse falloff and essentially no specular lobe**. Smooth-skinned Pals (`pw_09`, `pw_14`)
   get a wide, soft satin highlight — never a tight hotspot. There is no wetness, no subsurface glow,
   no clearcoat. A thin **warm rim light along the top-back edge** separates creature from background
   in almost every close shot; it is subtle (maybe 10–15% of the key's intensity) but always present.

5. **Creatures are saturated; the world is not.**
   Each creature is one saturated hero hue + a cream or white belly patch + a dark accent on the
   extremities. Grass sits at a **mid-value, moderately desaturated yellow-green**, rock is grey-green,
   sky is pale. The separation is done by *saturation contrast*, not value contrast and not outlines —
   a hot pink Pal at `pw_15` sits at almost the same luminance as the grass behind it and still pops.
   If you crank environment saturation to match the creature, the creature dies.

6. **Skies are high-key, thin, and wash out at the horizon.**
   Pale-to-mid cyan overhead, brightening to near-white at the horizon (`pw_02`, `pw_11`, `pw_13`).
   Clouds are **thin high-altitude wisps and veils**, low-contrast, never fluffy cumulus billboards
   and never a repeating cloud texture. The horizon band doing the washing-out is what sells scale.

7. **Distance is conveyed almost entirely by aerial perspective, and you can see kilometres.**
   Frames stack **three clear depth planes**: warm saturated foreground grass, mid-green mid-ground
   trees and rock, and far geometry lifted to a **pale blue-white flat silhouette** with its saturation
   crushed to near zero (`pw_11`'s tower, `pw_02`'s plateaus). There is always at least one large
   landmark at roughly 1–3 km reading as a nearly featureless pale shape. Fog is not a grey curtain —
   it is *hue-shifted toward the sky colour*, so distant terrain and sky converge.

8. **Ground is a dense variegated carpet with deliberate bald spots.**
   Grass is thousands of individual blade cards roughly **0.3–0.6 m tall**, varying in tint blade-to-blade
   and leaning coherently as if under one breeze. Crucially it is **not uniform**: worn dirt patches and
   paths cut through it (`pw_13`, `pw_15`), and flowers appear in **small clusters of 5–15 blooms** in a
   couple of spots per frame (`pw_11`, `pw_15`), never as an even scatter. Rocks are moss-capped on
   upward faces only. Uniform density is the single most common tell of a fake meadow.

9. **Shadows are soft, coloured, and anchored by a tight contact shadow.**
   Sun shadows have visibly soft edges and are only moderately dark — a **cooler, less saturated version
   of the lit colour, never black or grey**. What actually glues a creature to the ground is a small,
   tight, distinctly darker ellipse directly under the feet (`pw_15`, `pw_12`) with a hard-ish inner core
   and ~0.2 m of falloff. Crease occlusion (under chin, between limbs, where wool overlaps) is present
   but gentle. Drop the contact shadow and everything floats.

10. **Third-person camera: shoulder height, near-level, moderately wide, subject in the middle third.**
    The camera sits at roughly **1.6–1.8 m — at or just above the player's shoulder — pitched down only a
    few degrees**, so the horizon stays near the vertical centre of the frame (`pw_10`, `pw_11`, `pw_01`).
    FOV is moderately wide (**~55–70°**), enough to keep landscape either side of the subject without
    barrel distortion on the creature. The player occupies the lower-left or lower-right third, seen from
    mid-back up and cropped by the frame edge; the creature of interest sits at **4–10 m in the middle
    third**. Portraits break this and go longer-lens with visible background compression and DOF.

11. **One hero element, large calm areas around it.**
    A Palworld frame is a single high-detail focus plus generous low-frequency space — big flat sky, big
    flat grass, softly defocused background. In portraits the creature occupies **55–75% of frame height,
    roughly centred**, with everything behind it legible but low-detail (`pw_15`, `pw_12`, `pw_09`).
    Nothing in the background competes for the eye. Resist filling the frame edge-to-edge with props.

12. **Creatures are always caught mid-gesture, never in a neutral idle.**
    Even in a still, every Pal has an attitude: weight shifted onto one leg, one arm raised, head cocked,
    eyes closed in a grin, mid-leap (`pw_07`, `pw_12`, `pw_13`, `pw_15`). In multi-creature frames each
    individual is doing a *different* thing at a *different* facing — that variance is what separates a
    living meadow from a row of props. Pose them like animals with their own agenda, and never let two
    creatures share the same pose or the same orientation in one frame.
