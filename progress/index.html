Biggest remaining gaps

  • ALL THE DETAIL IS IN ONE PLANE. creature_portrait and interaction_feed exceed the edge ceiling from above. Per-tile, the bottom two rows of the frame run +2.5 to +10.8 over the reference plate while the top row runs up to -10 under it: a hard-silhouetted near-field grass carpet in front of an empty, fog-washed distance. Fixing it is not blurring the grass — the real plates carry plenty of near detail, theirs is merely softer and graded with depth.
  • EVERY SURFACE IS UNTEXTURED. Named by the r12 critic and still true; our best frame carries 725 distinct quantised colours against 1056 in the richest real plate.
  • FUR MICRODETAIL IS BLOCKED, not deferred: the fuzz map is being minified away, and creature_portrait has no edge headroom left to buy any with until the near field gives some back.
  • TERRAIN ACNE AT A GRAZING SUN needs a slope-scaled offset — polygonOffset on a customDepthMaterial. Neither shadow.bias (normalised depth) nor normalBias (a world-space push along the normal) can reach it.
  • taming.addTrust() is not on src/interaction's public surface. The correct shape is taming.addTrust(cr, -cost, 'broke-out') emitting taming:trust, so src/spheres stops reaching for internals.

r16 — The throw gets an arc — and the frame is measured to be over-sharp in one plane complete 2026-08-07
The throw animation was 60% dead time and 0% arc. Sampling the shoulder once per rendered frame caught it moving 156 degrees in a SINGLE step: the arm was behind the head in one frame and in front of the chest in the next, the sphere left the hand somewhere inside that gap, and the remaining 0.28 s crept from 86 to 33 degrees, which reads as lowering the arm rather than following through. Rebudgeted to 0.62 s across five keyframes, with the swing plane tilted ~35 degrees off vertical — a pure sagittal swing happens along the gameplay camera's view axis and projects to a short vertical smear, which is the third time that same trap has eaten an effect here. The round's second finding was only visible once the pose had real travel in it, and it is a good example of a verdict passing while the motion is wrong. The gesture clock DECAYED back to zero after finishing, and since the pose samples that clock, it replayed the whole animation in reverse at about 4x — the traveller un-threw the ball. No single reverse step crosses the 35 deg/frame teleport threshold the probe checks for, so it is a smooth, wrong motion rather than a jump, and the probe reported PASS.

Improvements landed

  • 0.16 s wind-up, 0.14 s of swing (about eight frames, the part that has to be legible), 0.12 s deceleration across the body, 0.20 s settling. The swing accelerates into the release at u^1.35, as steep as it can be without the peak frame going back over the teleport threshold.
  • The rest of the body throws too: chest leads, pelvis counter-rotates at 40%, weight crosses from the back foot to the front. No scripted step — foot placement belongs to the leg IK and a one-shot stride would fight it for the same two ankles.
  • Aiming is now half the wind-up: the sphere rides at the ear and slightly BEHIND the shoulder line rather than being presented at the chest, so the whole 0.62 s budget goes into the half of the arc worth watching, and the predicted-landing arc starts within centimetres of where the ball actually leaves the hand.
  • The gesture clock only ever runs forward now; a separate gestureOut fades authority out so a CANCELLED gesture releases the arm instead of rewinding it.
  • Measured after: 28.1 deg/frame peak, 9 frames between wind-up apex and release, tail frozen at the follow-through. The throw-to-capture harness stays at 12/12.

Gaps found by critics

  • TWO SHOTS ARE OVER-SHARP. creature_portrait 10.26 and interaction_feed 10.56 against an edge ceiling of 10.0. Tiling the frame 6x8 and computing measure.py's own quantity per tile localises it exactly: the bottom two rows (near grass) carry +2.5 to +10.8 per tile against the reference plate, while the top row is DOWN as much as -10. Every scrap of detail in the frame is crammed into one plane — a hard-edged near-field grass carpet — and there is almost none anywhere else. That is the same defect the r12 critic ranked second as 'no scene structure or depth layering', arriving this time as a number.
  • Still open from r12: every surface is untextured.

r15 — Vitals, creature health, the traveller's kit — and the facing bug complete 2026-08-06
Two merged fronts. The survival cluster the owner asked for, and the character finally being a character. The no-death rule was lifted by the owner for CREATURES only, and CLAUDE.md was rewritten to say so rather than leaving the next agent to find a contradiction. Two clocks, and the order between them is the whole design: stamina still runs out first and is still the capture window; health is the slower one. The player still collapses and never dies.

Improvements landed

  • Vitals (vigour, focus, stamina), and every one of them is spent by something. Focus is the meter that can REFUSE an action: below a throw's worth the sphere stays in the satchel. Measured through the real input path — 100 -> 36 stamina over a 4 s sprint, the fourth throw refused at 9.4 focus with the sphere count unmoved.
  • Creature health: a woolkin has 117 health and takes 6 per rifle round — exhaustion at shot 9, death at shot 19, so the capture window arrives ten shots before the kill. A body topples, lies 11 s, then SINKS rather than fades, because species materials are shared and fading a corpse fades every other woolkin in the meadow.
  • The facing bug. The rig faces -Z everywhere — movement basis, IK pole, stride, boot toes — but the torso and head were authored facing +Z. Since r13 the traveller had walked with the pack leading and the face trailing, and the follow camera sits behind the direction of travel, so it had been filming the character's FACE the whole time. A bald sphere is ambiguous enough that nobody caught it; a horned helmet with a nose guard is not.
  • The weapon's basis: the carry ANCHOR was body-relative while the carry DIRECTION came off the camera, so standing still and turning the view pivoted the weapon about the hand. Both are on the body now; only the aim direction comes off the camera. Sweeping the camera a full turn now gives 41.99 deg bore-to-body at all 16 samples, spread 0.00.
  • Horned iron helm and leather cuirass, with four negative results kept in the source: a lathe's normals follow its profile so a folded profile renders only backfaces; a partial torus ends at its own equator and its cut face is a flat disc; the face opening has to be wider than every bar crossing it; and the beard was built at r 0.084 around a 0.108 skull, i.e. entirely inside the head.

Gaps found by critics

  • The throw gesture existed but ran 0.45 s with only 0.054 s of forward swing — fixed in r16.

r14 — Berry bushes: grounded, smoothed, and the NaN normals that blacked them out complete 2026-08-06
The gatherables were the last thing still sitting on top of the image. They now publish 162 contact patches into the r13 ground field (-0.86/255 of ground luminance) and carry an object-side contact band of their own. Most of the round went on a bug that looked exactly like the feature. Several bushes rendered blue-black. Toggling the ground patches, swapping the material, and even setting the base colour to MAGENTA each changed the frame by zero bytes — which is the tell that the pixels are not coming from where you think. Mesh-hiding bisection over 175 meshes proved the bush painted them, and dumping the attributes found 2529 NaNs in a 3780-float normal array. smoothNormals() stored a base index (0, 3, 6...) into its accumulator and then multiplied it by 3 again on read, so every lookup ran past the end of the array. A NaN normal does not render as an obvious error; it renders as a surface the key light cannot reach, which is indistinguishable from contact shading cranked too far.

Improvements landed

  • Berry bushes and rocks grounded through world.setContactPatches('gatherables'), plus a per-material contact band anchored to the ground line.
  • Smooth-welded normals replace countable facets; berries are round instead of faceted polyhedra.
  • The NaN fix also removed the creature_portrait ratio 0.97 / edge 10.09 overshoots — they were the bug, not tuning. All six shots then passed every band.

Gaps found by critics

  • The captures/r14 directory on disk was later overwritten by a re-capture, so the passing r14 numbers no longer have frames behind them. Only the report survives.

r13 — Grounding — shadows, contact fields, and creatures that sit in the grass complete 2026-08-05
One gap, attacked from three sides at once, because the r11 and r12 critics both named the same thing first: nothing in our world meets the ground. Sky widened the shadow frustum and fixed the bias; world added a ground-side contact-occlusion field; creatures and the player got a stack of occluder slabs through the grass canopy. The round's lesson was a coordination one, and it is mine. All three levers darken ground pixels, and the ground/sky ratio band has almost no headroom on the low side. Three agents pushing the same number at once produced a collision none of them could attribute from inside their own directory. Net ground exposure is held neutral by two explicit counterweight constants in src/world rather than by moving a band.

Improvements landed

  • normalBias 0.12 -> 0.06 beat frustum size and cascade count COMBINED as the cause of 'nothing casts a shadow'. It is a world-space push along the normal and scales with nothing, so at 0.12 it walked every receiver 12 cm out from under its occluder — a fifth of the width off both sides of a 60 cm contact shadow. A COARSER shadow box with the lower bias out-measured a finer one by 34%.
  • src/world/contact.js: a ground-side contact-occlusion field in one draw call, patches sampled off the heightfield, multiply-blended into the HalfFloat target. Published as world.setContactPatches(tag, patches) so any system can ground its own props without importing anything.
  • A shadow map cannot ground a creature standing in tall grass. The carpet is 0.30-0.60 m and creatures stand IN it, so from any playable camera the pixels around a creature's base are grass, not ground: the diff image of a darkened ground plane is a comb of blade-shaped slivers nobody can see. Grounding had to become a stack of occluders THROUGH the canopy. Raising a single decal to clear the canopy detaches it from the feet before it clears.
  • Sphere break-out wariness inverted and fixed: a creature that broke out was ending up friendlier than before the throw. Second-throw odds after a wild break-out went 53.7% -> 18.5%.
  • The sphere's pull-in beam was authored along the flight line — and you throw at what the reticle is on, so the flight line IS the camera's forward axis. A cone down that axis projects to a bullseye: a glowing ring with no length near, a pale 30 px disc at range. Same family as the rim light that produced no rim.

Gaps found by critics

  • shadow.bias is normalised depth and normalBias is a world-space push; neither can fix terrain acne at a grazing sun. That needs a slope-scaled offset — polygonOffset on a customDepthMaterial.
  • Fur microdetail on creatures is blocked: the fuzz map is being minified away, and creature_portrait has no edge headroom to buy any with.

r12 — Near field, dusk, creature shading — and two new mechanics complete 2026-08-03
Split across two fronts. The first three builders answer the r11 critic directly: it ranked 'nothing in the in-dev world casts or receives light locally' as the single biggest gap, ahead of atmospheric depth and terrain variety. The other three add throw-to-capture and firearms. The design decision that makes the two new mechanics one feature rather than two: catch chance is read straight off the trust the berries bought. Feeding is not made obsolete by the sphere — it is what makes the sphere work. And the guns exist to weaken and stagger a creature so a sphere will hold it, which is the only framing in which firearms belong in a game about befriending things. There is no death anywhere in the build.

Improvements landed

  • Bond Spheres: aim arc showing the ACTUAL trajectory, ballistic throw, pull-in, 1-3 wobbles with rising tension, catch or break-out. Three independent RNG streams so adding a spark to the trail cannot change who gets caught.
  • Firearms with GTA's specific feel: aim as a state with an asymmetric 0.2s in / 0.45s out ramp, spread as a live number the reticle IS rather than a cosmetic bloom, recoil as two springs plus a leaky residual applied to the ROUND and not just the picture, phased interruptible reload, surface-aware impacts.
  • Loadout system: absolute slots 1-4 rather than a cycle, because a cycle key is miserable when one of four items is a weapon you did not mean to raise at a creature you were about to befriend. The swap is not instant and gates both consumers by construction.
  • Near field filled: the first 25 metres went from bare grass carpet to layered clumps, flowers and foreground detail.
  • Rocks stopped hanging in the sky. The faceted-banding pass in rockGeometry / boulderGeometry / stoneGeometry added a random phase `ph` before rounding y into bands and never subtracted it back out, so the phase became a translation and baked a constant +0.45*ph lift into the vertices — metres of rock in mid-air, in three files, written independently by three authors.
  • The instrument lesson from that bug: every object-space check EXONERATED it. Instance origins were correctly grounded (mean gap -0.15), the placement code queried heightAt properly, and five successive probes reported 'nothing floats' while the frame plainly showed boulders in the sky. It only falls out when you measure the GEOMETRY bounding box against the instance origin. The probes are kept as tools/_floatprobe*.mjs.
  • Weapons verified end-to-end: 36 of 36 assertions, including the one that matters — 26 hits drain stamina monotonically to zero, `creature:exhausted` fires exactly once, 15 further rounds are absorbed, population 17 -> 17, and the creature is still in the scene. Nothing died.
  • Sphere capture verified end-to-end: catch odds read straight off trust, measured at 0.078 wild / 0.426 after one berry / 0.895 after two. Feeding is what makes the sphere work.

Gaps found by critics

  • A creature that breaks out of a sphere currently ends up FRIENDLIER than before. The breakout subtracts 0.10 trust, but the ordinary proximity/patience taming arc keeps accruing while the player stands there and swamps it — measured 0.05 trust at throw, 0.32 after the break-out. A failed capture must be a setback the player can feel.
  • creature_portrait sits at ground/sky 0.97 against a 0.63-0.96 band. That band was measured off WIDE landscape plates and means little on a close-up; it is reported rather than silenced, and the band is left exactly as it was.
  • Palette depth still trails the reference: our best frame carries 760 distinct quantised colours against 1056 in the richest real plate.

r11 — Cast shadows actually render complete 2026-08-03
A blind critic wrote: 'Nothing in any of the five in-dev frames casts a shadow onto anything else, and that single absence produces most of the other symptoms the critics keep re-describing.' It was right, and the cause was not one bug but four, each measured before and after. The instructive part is what it was NOT: the shadow map was enabled, 2048x2048, with a valid texture, a correctly-following target and 79 casters. Every piece of the configuration was right and the feature still did nothing.

Improvements landed

  • Key/fill rebalanced. A shadow can only remove the key's contribution, and the unshadowed fill was out-contributing the key 2:1 on the ground (key removed 86.7 -> 63.4; fill removed 86.7 -> 36.6), capping any possible shadow at a quarter of the meadow. Key share 0.27 -> 0.372.
  • Sun arc flattened from 63 deg/^1.6 to 48 deg/^1.0. Four of five daylight shots had been lit from 33-51 deg, where a 12 m tree's 10 m shadow hides under its own canopy. Golden hour now lands in the 18-25 deg band.
  • Sun azimuth rotated 67 deg off the world's composition axis. Measured angle between camera forward and the sun had been 172.9 / 179.1 / 172.7 on three daylight shots — lit from behind the lens, which throws every shadow behind the object casting it.
  • Shadow frustum widened and the light pushed back to 620 m. A 144 m box at a 51 deg key covers 93 m of ground; the tree-covered hillside the critics kept naming sits at 150-300 m and had never been in the shadow map.
  • Terrain itself now casts (it never had), so ridges and banks lay their far sides into shade. Acne handled with a slope-scaled polygon offset via customDepthMaterial — shadow.bias is constant in depth and normalBias buys almost nothing at a grazing sun.

Gaps found by critics

  • The diff is 0.773/255 against an 8/255 target. Proven by experiment to be a content problem, not a lighting one: shrinking the shadow box to 30 m, which puts every texel on the near field at 1.5 cm each, made it worse.
  • Grass casting was tried and measured as a null result. Recorded in the source rather than silently dropped.
