Biggest remaining gaps

  • NOTHING MEETS THE GROUND. The r11 critic's decisive cue in the creature-group pair: our foreground bush is 'an icosphere with berries as separate spheres stuck to it, sitting on the grass with no contact shadow and no ambient occlusion at the ground line. It doesn't sit in the world, it sits on top of the image.' The same defect names the creature portrait — a hard black elliptical blob decal instead of an occlusion gradient.
  • CREATURES ARE LIT FROM EVERY DIRECTION EQUALLY. 'The underside of the creature is the same value as the top.' Prime suspect is the fur wrap term (0.60), which remaps a fully back-facing surface up to 0.375 and flattens a sphere into a disc. The designs themselves were praised — 'it's the rendering that fails it, not the design.'
  • DUSK IS A DAY RENDER WITH THE EXPOSURE PULLED. Independently reached by the r11 critic and by the guardrails from the other side: ground/sky 0.99 against the reference plate's 1.27. 'A night scene with no light sources in it. The fireflies are unlit point sprites contributing nothing... the brightest thing in frame is the HUD.'
  • THE FIRST 25 METRES ARE EMPTY. Per-band shadow coverage down the over-shoulder frame reads 0.011 / 0.042 / 0.186 / 0.050 / 0.000 / 0.027 from sky to nearest. Mid-distance works; the near meadow is bare grass carpet. Same root cause as the flat palette (337 colours against a 420-1100 band) and the low local contrast (5.06 against real plates at 8.06-9.87).
  • OUR POST IS NOT READING AT ALL. 'Uniformly, mercilessly sharp from the grass blade at the camera to the peak on the horizon', 'no aerial perspective — the terrain collapses into a stack of flat cutouts'. We ship FogExp2, aerial-perspective materials AND a DOF pass, so this is a strength-of-effect problem, not a missing feature — which is worse, because it means we are paying for all three and reading as if we had none.
  • UNTEXTURED GREY PROPS SIT AT FOCAL POINTS. The stone arch 'reads as unshipped greybox: a placeholder blockout that never got a material pass, placed exactly where the eye lands'. Contrast the reference: 'cliff faces carry layered strata with moss accumulating only on upward-facing ledges — a material that knows which way gravity points.' The arch is a landmark in world/index.js and is not yet assigned.
  • TERRAIN IS ONE MATERIAL AT ONE SCALE. The same ground mottle and grass card run from camera to horizon with no density falloff, no dirt paths, no rock or sand transitions. 'Real ground tells you where people walk.' Not yet assigned.
  • Reference gap: only 1 of 17 official stills is early-game peaceful exploration, so interaction_feed has no honest comp.
  • Reference gap: the trailer never shows a creature noticing an approaching player, so behaviour_approach is graded against the 12 written criteria.

r11 — Cast shadows actually render complete 2026-08-03
A blind critic wrote: 'Nothing in any of the five in-dev frames casts a shadow onto anything else, and that single absence produces most of the other symptoms the critics keep re-describing.' It was right, and the cause was not one bug but four, each measured before and after. The instructive part is what it was NOT: the shadow map was enabled, 2048x2048, with a valid texture, a correctly-following target and 79 casters. Every piece of the configuration was right and the feature still did nothing.

Improvements landed

  • Key/fill rebalanced. A shadow can only remove the key's contribution, and the unshadowed fill was out-contributing the key 2:1 on the ground (key removed 86.7 -> 63.4; fill removed 86.7 -> 36.6), capping any possible shadow at a quarter of the meadow. Key share 0.27 -> 0.372.
  • Sun arc flattened from 63 deg/^1.6 to 48 deg/^1.0. Four of five daylight shots had been lit from 33-51 deg, where a 12 m tree's 10 m shadow hides under its own canopy. Golden hour now lands in the 18-25 deg band.
  • Sun azimuth rotated 67 deg off the world's composition axis. Measured angle between camera forward and the sun had been 172.9 / 179.1 / 172.7 on three daylight shots — lit from behind the lens, which throws every shadow behind the object casting it.
  • Shadow frustum widened and the light pushed back to 620 m. A 144 m box at a 51 deg key covers 93 m of ground; the tree-covered hillside the critics kept naming sits at 150-300 m and had never been in the shadow map.
  • Terrain itself now casts (it never had), so ridges and banks lay their far sides into shade. Acne handled with a slope-scaled polygon offset via customDepthMaterial — shadow.bias is constant in depth and normalBias buys almost nothing at a grazing sun.

Gaps found by critics

  • The diff is 0.773/255 against an 8/255 target. Proven by experiment to be a content problem, not a lighting one: shrinking the shadow box to 30 m, which puts every texel on the near field at 1.5 cm each, made it worse.
  • Grass casting was tried and measured as a null result. Recorded in the source rather than silently dropped.
